An `invoke` names each instruction's accounts. You rarely need all of them:
Arete fills every account it can work out, and tells you how to find the
rest. You need no SDK or typed client for this. `sandbox_rules` and the
preview's refusals tell you everything below.

## What Arete fills

Every account in `sandbox_rules` may carry a `fill`. Leave those accounts
out and Arete fills them when it builds the transaction:

| `fill`    | Arete fills it with                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `fixed`   | A fixed address: the IDL's own, a sysvar (`rent`, `clock`, the instructions sysvar), a native program (`system_program`, `associated_token_program`, `memo_program`), or the program itself                                                                                                                            |
| `wallet`  | The sandbox wallet, the only signer                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `derived` | A PDA or known address from the program's spec; Anchor's event authority; a token program, as the owner of the mint it goes with (`base_token_program` with `base_mint`); or a token account of yours, as the wallet's associated token account for the mint it goes with (`user_base_token_account` with `base_mint`) |
| `read`    | A field of another account the instruction names, read from the chain when the transaction is built: `pool.base_mint`                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

A filled account that the IDL doesn't explain also has a `source`, such as
`pool.base_mint` or `the token program that owns base_mint`.

Fills depend on each other. `base_mint` is read from the `pool`, then
`user_base_token_account` and `base_token_program` follow from `base_mint`.
Give the accounts a chain starts from and Arete fills the rest in one go.

An account you give is never replaced. The preview lists every account
Arete filled (`instructions[].filled`), so you can check them. Fills are
worked out from account names and the program's spec, so read the preview
before you invoke.

## The accounts you give

An account with no `fill` is yours to give. In `sandbox_rules` it has a
`lookup` with what Arete knows about it:

| Field             | Meaning                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `how`             | One sentence on how to find it                                                                                                |
| `docs`            | What the program's IDL says about it, such as a PDA's seeds the spec can't express                                            |
| `accountType`     | The program account type its name points to (`Pool`, `LbPair`, `Reserve`)                                                     |
| `discriminator`   | That type's discriminator (base58), for a `getProgramAccounts` `memcmp` at offset 0                                           |
| `findBy`          | Fields of that type at fixed byte offsets, to search by: `[{ "field": "base_mint", "offset": 43 }]`                           |
| `heldBy`          | Account types of the program with a field naming it (`GlobalConfig.protocol_fee_recipients`): read one and take it from there |
| `tokenAccountFor` | For a token account of yours: the mints it may be for                                                                         |
| `unlocks`         | The accounts Arete fills once you give this one                                                                               |

Usually you give only the account an action starts from: a pool, a pair or
a reserve. Ways to find it:

- **From what you already know.** You found the pool in a quote, an API
  response, an earlier transaction or an account you read. Read accounts
  with `program_read_account` to follow their fields.
- **From a stack.** A stack that lists the program's pools or markets gives
  their addresses. You can read it through the sandbox when the sandbox's
  rules allow every program it decodes. See
  [Reading in a sandbox](/sandboxes/read-scope/).
- **By searching the program.** With your own RPC, run `getProgramAccounts`
  on the program with a `memcmp` filter for the `discriminator` at offset 0
  and one for each `findBy` field you know, such as the coin's mint.
- **From the protocol.** Use its app, API, SDK or docs.

Token accounts of yours that Arete can't pair with a mint (`user_token_in`
on a two-sided pool) are usually your associated token accounts. Derive
them with `sandbox_derive` (`kind: "ata"`), and create them with the
baseline `createIdempotent` in the same action if they don't exist.

## Refusals

A preview or invoke that is missing accounts is refused with
`sandbox-intent-invalid`. The message lists the accounts you must find
first, each with its `how`, then the accounts Arete fills once those are
given, then anything it couldn't derive and why. The refusal also carries:

- `missingAccounts`: every missing account.
- `filledAccounts`: what Arete already filled.
- `accountGuidance`: one entry per missing account. For an account you
  must find, the entry is its `lookup`. For an account Arete will fill, it
  has `waitingOn`, which says what it is waiting for (`Arete reads it from
pool.base_mint once pool is given`).

Give the first group, preview again, and repeat until it passes.

## Worked examples

The exact accounts come from `sandbox_rules` for your sandbox. These show
the shape.

### PumpSwap buy

PumpSwap (`pAMMBay6oceH9fJKBRHGP5D4bD4sWpmSwMn52FMfXEA`) `buy` takes 23
accounts plus the remaining accounts PumpSwap reads after them. You give
two:

- `pool`: a `Pool` account. Its `findBy` includes `base_mint` at offset 43
  and `quote_mint` at offset 75, so a coin's pool is a `getProgramAccounts`
  search for the Pool discriminator and the coin's mint at offset 43.
- `global_config`: PumpSwap's `GlobalConfig` account.

From the pool Arete reads `base_mint`, `quote_mint` and the pool's two token
accounts. It picks a protocol fee recipient from
`global_config.protocol_fee_recipients` and derives your two token
accounts, both token programs and the program's PDAs. It also appends the
remaining accounts PumpSwap needs, such as `pool_v2` and the buyback fee
accounts.

```json
{
  "action": "invoke",
  "args": {
    "instructions": [
      {
        "program": "PumpSwap",
        "instruction": "buy",
        "args": { "base_amount_out": "1000000", "max_quote_amount_in": "10000000" },
        "accounts": { "pool": "<pool>", "global_config": "<global config>" }
      }
    ]
  }
}
```

Your quote token account must hold the SOL you spend, as wrapped SOL.
Create and fund it in the same action with the baseline `createIdempotent`,
a System `transfer` and `syncNative`.

### Meteora DLMM swap

Meteora DLMM (`LBUZKhRxPF3XUpBCjp4YzTKgLccjZhTSDM9YuVaPwxo`) `swap2`: you
give `lb_pair`, an `LbPair` account, and your two token accounts,
`user_token_in` and `user_token_out`. Their `tokenAccountFor` is
`token_x_mint` or `token_y_mint`, depending on which way you swap. Arete
reads both reserves, both mints and the oracle from the pair, derives both
token programs, and chooses the bin arrays the swap crosses from the pair's
current active bin when the transaction is built. Bin arrays from an
earlier quote go stale, so leave them out.

### Kamino Lend deposit, with Kamino Farms

A Kamino Lend deposit runs several instructions in one action: refresh the
reserve, refresh the obligation, then deposit. Its first deposit also
creates the user metadata, the obligation, and the obligation's farm state.
Enable Kamino Farms in your rules too, because a deposit into a reserve
with a farm calls it.

For `depositReserveLiquidityAndObligationCollateral` you give:

- `reserve`: the `Reserve` for the token you deposit. Its `findBy` includes
  `liquidity.mintPubkey`, the token's mint.
- `obligation`: your obligation in that market. Kamino derives it from your
  wallet and the market. Its address is in Kamino's SDK and docs, and in
  your earlier transactions.
- `reserveDestinationDepositCollateral`: the reserve's collateral supply
  vault. Read the reserve with `program_read_account` and take
  `collateral.supplyVault`.

Arete reads `lendingMarket`, `reserveLiquidityMint`,
`reserveCollateralMint` and `reserveLiquiditySupply` from the reserve, and
derives `lendingMarketAuthority`, your source token account for the
reserve's mint, both token programs and the instructions sysvar.

The farm instructions need the reserve's farm. That is `farmCollateral` on
the reserve you read, plus your obligation's user state in that farm and
the Kamino Farms program. `sandbox_rules` lists them under each
instruction with a `lookup`. If the deposit's farm accounts are missing,
the refusal names them.

## When an account can't be found this way

Some accounts depend on live state or on the way you trade: the tick
arrays near a pool's current price, or which vault is the input. Names
can't say which, so these stay yours. Take them from the protocol's quote,
SDK or API, close to when you send the action. For a Jupiter-routed swap,
Jupiter's `/swap-instructions` gives every account of the route. See
[Actions](/sandboxes/actions/#swaps).
